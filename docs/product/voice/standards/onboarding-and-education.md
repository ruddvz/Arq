# Onboarding and education language

Arq onboarding teaches the product's mental model, not a marketing story.

## First concepts to teach

In this order where relevant:

1. one semantic project, several views;
2. Design, Document, Inspect, Review and Present are modes over the same project;
3. a preview is not committed work;
4. local save and remote sync are separate;
5. `.arq` compatibility, opening, working copy and publication are different stages;
6. imports can preserve, convert, approximate, flatten, omit or retain data;
7. validation can block a change without changing the previous project state;
8. AI proposals are reviewable proposals, not hidden edits.

## Do not teach shortcuts as concepts

Teach the action first, then the shortcut.

Good:

> Select Wall in Draw. With a keyboard, press W.

Bad:

> Press W to start Arq.

## Progressive disclosure

Do not explain all BIM semantics to a first-time user. Introduce terminology at the moment the underlying distinction affects a decision.

## Example projects and fixtures

Label demo, sample, tutorial and fixture data honestly. Never allow a synthetic benchmark count or demo object to look like user project data.

## Empty states

An empty state should explain why the surface is empty and name the next meaningful action. Do not fill it with invented metrics, fake activity, sample collaborators, placeholder issues or fake model health.

## Educational claims

Do not imply that learning Arq certifies architectural competence or substitutes for required software in a course or practice.
