# Competitive gaps and ARQ position

**Status:** Research and proposal input. External sources do not establish current ARQ implementation truth.

## Scope

This synthesis compares drawing databases, BIM authoring systems, product CAD, cloud CAD, scene composition, visual delivery, geospatial streaming, and open exchange.

## Verified source observations

- Existing systems are usually strongest in one or two layers: drawing continuity, BIM semantics, exact geometry, feature history, cloud collaboration, scene composition, or large-scale streaming.
- No public standard reviewed here combines local-first portable project ownership, exact semantic operations, multi-domain capability negotiation, deterministic evidence, truthful approximation reporting, AI proposal governance, and federated scale in one format.
- That absence is an opportunity, not proof that combining all layers is easy or desirable in one implementation milestone.

## Lessons for `.arq`

- ARQ should be modular: a small stable core, registered capabilities, domain packs, adapter contracts, and conformance profiles.
- ARQ should lead with inspectable truth and recoverability before breadth.
- ARQ should prove architecture first, then a small mechanical assembly, then larger product and city federation slices.

## Gaps ARQ can address

- Visible fidelity reports can become a product differentiator.
- Revision-pinned AI proposals can make automation safer than direct script mutation.
- Portable publications plus optional collaboration can avoid forced cloud ownership.
- Kernel-neutral semantics can reduce long-term vendor lock-in.
